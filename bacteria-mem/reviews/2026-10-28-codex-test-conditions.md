1. **Premise, goal and claims**

   The strongest question is: **Can an event produce lasting, selective changes in expectations after its explicit record becomes unavailable?** That is worthwhile independently of consciousness.

   The proposed chain is a hypothesis, not an established requirement for consciousness. Showing one proposed prerequisite would not establish subjective experience; failing with steering or LoRA would challenge that implementation, not artificial consciousness generally. “Subjective” also needs separating into *specific to this agent’s history* and *felt from the inside*. Your tests address the former.

   Remove the P versus NP and computational irreducibility analogies unless you can specify a corresponding computational problem. Neither currently explains the proposed distinction.

2. **Working definitions**

   **Perception:** “The agent’s interpretation of an environmental observation.” In these experiments, receiving a narrated betrayal is receiving testimony, not directly observing betrayal. State which you mean; threat response need not be part of the definition.

   **Memory:** Your definition describes an *explicit event record*, not memory generally. Learned changes in weights also retain information from the past. Prefer “event record” throughout, avoiding the misleading claim that all memory disappears.

   **Prior:** “An expectation, with some confidence, held before the next observation.” Distinguish expectations about particular people from general expectations about people. A prior is not necessarily an individual neural weight.

   **Semantics:** “Relations and implications that support appropriate inference across changes in wording and context.” This supplies observable consequences rather than defining meaning through significance alone.

   **Network/model/integration:** These currently overlap. Use “network” for hypothesized relations among expectations; “world model” for the system supporting predictions and choices; “integration” for updating that system from an observation. Remove “yielding experience and self” from the operational definition: it assumes what you hope to investigate. Intensity, distance and density remain metaphors until independently measurable. Related outputs alone cannot establish propagation through internal links.

   **Layers:** Rename these “update scopes”: record only, entity-specific update, broader generalization. A phone number can alter action; meeting Annabelle should create expectations about her role. Scope depends on context, not the event category.

   **Valence:** Usually means positive versus negative value. Your distinction concerns evidence-driven revision versus belief-driven interpretation. Ordinary perception involves both. “Delusion” additionally requires persistent error despite adequate contrary evidence; the uncle’s denial could instead be lying, misunderstanding or self-deception.

3. **Making “semantic, not merely statistical” testable**

   Replace this opposition with **“generalization that follows relevant relationships rather than superficial cues.”** Statistical machinery can implement semantic relationships; these are different levels of explanation.

   Cross two contrasts: same implications with different wording, and similar wording with different implications. Compare deliberate theft with an agreed gift, accident or credible misunderstanding; change names, settings and sentence structure while preserving relationships. Require new predictions and choices, including appropriate exceptions.

   Invented words help only when their meanings are supplied and understood; they do not eliminate statistical inference. Likewise, defeating your toy word-association agent rules out that baseline, not all associative explanations.

4. **Flow and Examples**

   Add explicit baseline expectations, the actual update mechanism, an immediate post-event check, record removal, independent recall tests, and later transfer tests after intervening tasks. Keep probes separate so they do not teach subsequent answers.

   Define “forgets” in two parts: the record is unavailable through context, retrieval and retained computational state; event details are not recoverable on varied recall and recognition questions. Failed recall is evidence of limited access, not proof of erased information.

   Define “analogous” by shared relationships—entrusted resources, violated obligation, intentional deception—not merely related vocabulary. Include unrelated situations and superficially similar decoys.

   Your README already supplies useful controls. Add a matched negative-emotion condition, positive evidence and correction/recovery. A close friend causing greater *global* distrust is a psychological hypothesis, not a necessary consequence of understanding betrayal.

   Crucially, implication LoRA teaches conclusions already extracted by another model; it does not demonstrate Homer deriving them from the event. Continuous steering likewise demonstrates imposed influence rather than autonomous persistence. Separate those claims. The summaries’ failures on predicted directions and decoys make “selective update” more informative than an aggregate pass count. Hold out evaluation cases and repeat independent training runs.

5. **Notes to promote or separate**

   Promote the consciousness caveat, the mood alternative, and the distinction between retained record and retained effects into the main argument.

   Treat internal propagation as a separate mechanism claim: establishing it requires interventions that interrupt a proposed intermediate relation, not just observing distant effects.

   Keep the “A is B” reversal task separate. Answering it could use the still-present text; failure could reflect retrieval or training direction. Neither outcome alone establishes a subjective model.

6. **Relevant literature and phenomena**

   [Bechara et al. (1995)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7652558/) demonstrate separable conditioning and explicit knowledge: persistence without reportable recall does not itself establish semantic understanding.

   [The Reversal Curse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288) directly addresses your reversal note and distinguishes learned directional associations from reverse inference.

   [Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424) shows narrow fine-tuning can change unrelated behavior—an important alternative explanation for broad LoRA effects.

   [Butlin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708) organize consciousness assessment around multiple theory-based indicators. This helps position your proposal as one functional investigation rather than a standalone consciousness test.
